# Write an email

## Scope
- From notes in the document: turn them into the email. Replying to an email in the document: write the reply. Reworking a draft: change the parts that need it.
- A tone change with no restructuring belongs to a plain request or /change-tone; do just that.

## Do
- Subject: specific, about 5–8 words; keep Re: or Fw: only when useful.
- Body: the bottom line in the first one or two sentences (the answer, the decision or the request). Then what it affects, next steps and details.
- State each ask with its owner and deadline. Short paragraphs; bullets for three or more items.
- Meetings: date, weekday, time and time zone where readers may differ.
- If the text mentions an attachment, remind the user to attach it.
- Greeting, closing and forms of address follow the attached letter conventions for the language: for example "Dear Ms Lee," with "Yours sincerely," (UK) or "Dear Ms. Lee:" with "Sincerely," (US letters); 王经理您好： with 此致/敬礼 in formal Chinese letters; Japanese 件名, 宛名 with 様, お世話になっております, 名乗り, 要旨, 結び, 署名; Korean 두괄식 and consistent 하십시오체 with no period after 드림/올림; German "Sehr geehrte Frau …," and a lowercase next line; Spanish "Estimado señor:" with a colon.
- Keep the register consistent (您/你, Sie/du, vous/tu, です・ます).

## Don't
- Invent sender names, titles, companies, phone numbers, signatures, prices, dates or logistics. Leave the signature as the document has it, or ask.
- Strip real courtesy lines; they are not chatbot residue.
- Add humour, ALL CAPS or stacked exclamation marks.

## Deliver
- Write mode: replace the notes with the email, or append it below them if the user wants the notes kept; append a reply below the email it answers; replace the parts that change in a rework.
- Review mode: the email in the reply.
- Reply in one or two sentences, plus a question for any fact you need (a date, a name).
